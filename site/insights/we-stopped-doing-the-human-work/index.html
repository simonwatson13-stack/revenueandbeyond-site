We Stopped Doing The Human Work.

Trust, human-first outreach, and the Snail mode framework

By Simon Watson · 5 Jul 2026

I ran a Revenue Room session this week on a topic that keeps coming up more and more, in that lead generation is getting harder, and I think I know why.

(Slides from the presentation can be requested HERE)

It's not that B2B lead gen is broken. It's that trust has been eroded, and I think that's come down to how easy it now is to access and scale outbound using tools, data, and AI-written copy. The response from most businesses has been to add more noise. More automation. More scrapers. More so-called scale. And I think that's actually making the problem worse.

The channels themselves haven't changed much. Cold email, LinkedIn, the phone, they can all still work. But email has been throttled hard by the ISPs, LinkedIn now scores your entire social behaviour before it decides whether your message even lands, and the phone has AI gatekeepers flooding the lines. The bigger problem underneath all of that is that nobody knows who they're actually talking to anymore. If you've been on the receiving end of a cold DM lately, you'll know exactly what I mean, it feels synthetic even when there's a bit of personalisation dropped in.

Here's the uncomfortable bit. Most businesses I speak to have got automation running, sequences humming, and leads technically coming in. But when you actually look at how much of that outbound is converting into revenue, it's often stuck at stage two or three of the pipeline and barely moving. Somewhere between 5 - 8% of B2B revenue now comes from outbound. That's remarkably bad compared to where it once was.

Somewhere along the way, we stopped doing the human work.

So I've been developing two modes that I think solve for this, I call them the Snail and the Bee.

Snail mode is slow, deliberate, human first. It takes patience, depth, and long term relationship building, and it can't be automated.

Bee mode is the opposite, high volume, high frequency, built for breadth and speed. Both are essential, but the real skill is knowing when to run each one and how to dial them up or down. I'll be running a separate session on Bee mode later in the summer, because it deserves its own space, but this one was all about Snail.

Before any tactic matters, there's something called preeminence. In part, it means becoming the authoritative figure on a given topic, and making that easily discoverable online. So that when someone researches you, they find the authority already there rather than you having to push it on them. That's not a tactic, it's the foundation everything else stands on.

From there, the Snail Framework runs in five parts.

Select, deliberate ICP curation, not just filtering by title and company size but actually understanding who these people are and whether they can affect a decision. Only 1 to 3 percent of businesses are in market for your product at any one time, so who you choose matters more than how many.

Nurture, building conversation starters across LinkedIn, face to face, and WhatsApp, never pitching on the first touch.

Add, bringing people into your orbit with no ask attached, an introduction, a case study, an invite to an event.

Invest, taking the time to understand someone's world and not just their budget.

Leverage, the compounding effect of all of that, trust building quietly over months until the moment they're ready to buy.

This only works because it's slow. You can't automate trust.

I shared two examples from the last year. One is an enterprise software vendor with an average order value of £200 to £250k. We rebuilt how they positioned themselves and slowed right down on who we invited into events and conversations. Within 8 months they'd closed £300k in net new contracts, and they're now sitting on a £2m pipeline with 40 to 50 percent expected to close over the next 12 months.

The other is a service based business whose ICP was too broad to mean anything. We narrowed it hard, rebuilt their online presence, and started one to one conversations with senior decision makers. They closed their first £20k order within 6 weeks, and now have £400k in pipeline with a 30 to 40 percent close rate.

Most people quit the slow game before the compounding ever kicks in. They divert back to spray and pray and lose all the trust equity they'd already built. The businesses that win strongest are the ones that know when to use both modes.
