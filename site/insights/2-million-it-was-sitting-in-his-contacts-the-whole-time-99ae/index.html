£2 million. It was sitting in his contacts the whole time.

The "a-ha" moments that can transform a clients business is one of the most satisfying parts of this job.

By Simon Watson · 7 Jun 2026

Some of the most satisfying moments in this line of work are when a client has an "aha" moment.

Sometimes it's something they've known and subsequently forgotten. Sometimes it's something they've never known but realise is fairly obvious once it's staring them straight in the face.

During the earlier stages of working with founders, I try to understand the elements of the business that are creating bottlenecks as it relates to sales. From there, we begin to build a commercial layer that allows them to free themselves up to work on the business instead of in the business.

A common theme is understanding deal flow and where leads are ultimately coming from.

As they say, “a healthy pipeline cures all ills”.

If you've got a team of salespeople or account managers struggling to fill pipe, and as a founder you're sat there with a wealth of knowledge, historical contacts, and relationships that have either never been approached or were approached in the early days and haven't been touched since, then this is often an area that yields some quick wins and, dare I say it, low-hanging fruit.

This has happened on a number of occasions recently, but it reminds me most of the time I was working with Simon Wardropper at Realtime Agency. Simon hired me to come in and reduce the bottleneck and reliance on his personal contribution to sales.

Simon has a wealth of contacts from his well-documented history in the marketing sector. People and brands across telco, banking, insurance, consumer businesses. A real black book that most people would kill for.

During my first few sessions with him, we looked at a strategy to unpick his contacts and build a soft outreach approach that would engage people in meaningful, warm conversations rather than just pitching.

That led to a pipeline of contacts genuinely interested in learning more about the agency's services, and in some cases, ready to procure. Over the following six months, we generated over £2 million in new pipeline. Hidden inside his head, his WhatsApp, and his LinkedIn profile.

So I put it to you... When was the last time you looked at your own contacts and put together a sequence of events to reconnect with people you've worked with in the past? Ex-colleagues, ex-bosses, ex-partners, ex-employees — many of whom will have stepped up the career ladder and are now in more senior roles.

If you haven't done this in a while, here's what I'd recommend. Download your LinkedIn contacts, filter by sector and industry, scan through them with intention, and build a meaningful, systemised outreach approach. Involve one of your more trusted salespeople. As the conversations and relationships start to rebuild, hand them over to that person to nurture and follow through into new business. Stay close as a guardian, but let your team do the heavy lifting and the closing.

These are the kinds of projects I genuinely love getting involved in. The pipeline was always there. It just needed someone to go looking for it.

Let me know if any of you decide to put this into practice over the coming weeks. I'd love to hear the results.
