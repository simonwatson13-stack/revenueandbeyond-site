The job I had to stop doing

Letter 03. Clients thought they were hiring me, not the agency.

By Simon Watson · 25 Oct 2026

In the early days of the agency, I was in every pitch.

And I soon realised something. The clients thought they were hiring me, not the agency.

That sounds like a compliment. It isn't. If clients are hiring you, the agency can only grow as fast as you can get to the next meeting.

I needed to get out of the way, and fast.

So I brought in a sales team. I stayed involved in the initial pitches, but as soon as it came to building proposals and structuring the deals, I let the team take care of it and got out of the way. I came back in for the final checks, and if it was a really big client, for the close.

Creating that space between me and the prospect meant the agency could grow faster.

I see the same shape in most of the founder-led businesses I work with now. The jobs founders struggle to give up are rarely the ones they hate. Those go first. The hard ones are the jobs you're best at. The ones that feel like "you". The pitch, the big client, the final check on every piece of work.

"Being good at it is exactly why it's still on your desk."

Nobody else gets the chance to learn it, so nobody else can do it, so it stays with you. And every hour it takes is an hour you're not spending on the work only you can do.

What are you still doing because you're good at it, not because it's yours to do?
